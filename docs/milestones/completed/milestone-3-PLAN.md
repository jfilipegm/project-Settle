## Goal

Give Settle one coherent, deliberate design before the household features
multiply the screens: the design system (tokens, type, icons, components,
motion, house rules) in code and in `docs/DESIGN.md`, every existing
screen rebuilt on it, and the whole interface in English and European
Portuguese. Settle stays **one responsive web app** (`PRODUCT.md`,
Platform): the quick split is tuned first for phone width, the household
for desktop width, and every screen works at every width from 360 px.

**What the user sees at the end:** the same features as today (the bill
splitter, receipt scanning, the receipt check, Review lines, Settings),
reorganised and restyled. The split becomes three named steps at phone
width (Receipt, Who had what, The split), with the live split beside the
items on a wide screen. A person is chosen once and their items are
tapped. The app follows the browser's language (English or Portuguese)
until the user picks one in Settings. Light and dark themes both follow
the new palette.

**What does not change:** the split maths, the bill model and its saved
draft, the receipt reader and parser, the CSP and the privacy guarantees.
No feature is added or removed, except the outdated Finances placeholder,
which is replaced by a Household placeholder (S8).

## Decisions taken in this plan

**S1. The palette** (light, then dark; every role identical in both):

| Role | Light | Dark |
|------|-------|------|
| Ground (page) | `#ECEEF1` | `#12141C` |
| Card / surface | `#FFFFFF` | `#1C1F2B` |
| Line | `#DCE0E5` | `#2E3240` |
| Control border | `#C9CED6` | `#3A3F4E` |
| Quiet text | `#565C6B` | `#A3A9B7` |
| Ink (text, primary button) | `#191C27` | `#EEF0F4` |
| Rust accent (links, focus, active tab, category bars) | `#B83F1E` | `#F07A52` |
| Success text / fill | `#1F6B44` on `#E3F1E8` | `#8FD3AC` on `#17382A` |
| Warning text / fill | `#7A5208` on `#FBF0D9` | `#F0C46A` on `#3A2E12` |
| Error text / fill | `#A3221B` on `#FBE4E1` | `#F4A39A` on `#3D1C1A` |

One accent per screen. Primary buttons are ink (light) or near-white
(dark), never rust. Green, amber and red belong to the receipt check and
errors only, always with an icon and words. `THEME_COLORS` (the
`theme-color` meta) becomes the header colour: `#ffffff` light and
`#1c1f2b` dark, kept in step by the existing tests.

**S2. People colours.** Six, in this fixed order: indigo, teal, raspberry,
violet, olive, lake. Light: `#3D57A3`, `#1D7268`, `#A3365A`, `#6A4C98`,
`#56651A`, `#2C6585`, each with a white initial (all ≥ 5.7:1). Dark:
`#7F97E0`, `#45B3A3`, `#DB7398`, `#A18BD6`, `#A3B55A`, `#6AA8CB`, each
with a dark (`#12141C`) initial (all ≥ 6.0:1). A person's colour comes
from their current position in the bill's people (index mod 6), so it
needs no stored field. It is deterministic from the current order, not
fixed for the life of a bill: removing a person shifts the colour of
everyone after them (O-EXT-1). That is acceptable because the colour is
never the only identifier. A colour never appears without the
initial or the name, and never means good or bad. A bill can hold more
than six people (`LIMITS.maxPeople`), so colours repeat from the seventh
person; that is acceptable because the initial and name always come
with the colour. The CSP has `style-src 'self'` without
`'unsafe-inline'`, so per-element values (a person's colour, a share
bar's width) are set through React's `style` prop, which writes the
CSSOM and is allowed, usually as a CSS custom property
(`--person-color`, `--share`), never as a `style="…"` string in markup;
the page-load request check, which fails on any CSP violation, guards
it (L2-O1).

**S3. Type.** Three faces, self-hosted:

- **Unbounded** 500 and 600: page titles, card titles, the one key number
  on a screen. Never body text or buttons, never 700+.
- **JetBrains Mono** 400 and 500: every amount, date and count, with
  tabular figures.
- **Source Sans 3** 400, 600 and 700: everything else.

A scale of 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 / 16 / 17 / 19 / 22 / 28 / 40 px (as rem
tokens), body 16 px, line height 1.5 for text and 1.1 for titles.

**S4. Fonts are bundled, not fetched.** From the `@fontsource` npm
packages (`@fontsource/unbounded`, `@fontsource/jetbrains-mono`,
`@fontsource/source-sans-3`, all SIL OFL 1.1), static weights only,
imported for the latin and latin-ext subsets only (Portuguese needs
latin; latin-ext covers names). Vite emits the `woff2` files into the
build, so they come from the app's own origin and the CSP's
`font-src 'self'` holds unchanged. `font-display: swap`. Budget: the
woff2 files a first page view downloads total under 250 KB; over
400 KB for all of them is a stop condition. The licences join
`app/public/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

**S5. One icon set.** `@tabler/icons-react` (MIT), each icon imported by
name so only the used ones ship, through one `Icon` wrapper fixing the
stroke at 1.75 and the size at 16, 20 or 24 px; decorative icons are
`aria-hidden`, icon-only buttons carry an `aria-label`. The "⚠ Check"
glyph and every other Unicode stand-in become Tabler icons.

**S6. Shape, depth and motion.** Corner radii 8 (chips), 12 (controls)
and 16 (cards), nothing else. Cards sit flat on a 1 px line; only menus
and sheets are raised, with a shadow tinted from the ink. A pressed
button scales to 0.97 in 140 ms; anything that appears uses one ease-out,
`cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`, in 150 to 250 ms; nothing animates on
page load; an item row that gains or loses a person flashes for 200 ms.
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` replaces movement with an instant
change. The browser's own parts take the palette too: focus ring (2 px
rust, 2 px offset), text selection, caret and `accent-color`.

**S7. House rules** (from the canvas; `docs/DESIGN.md` keeps them): no
gradient text, buttons or bars; no coloured stripe on a card edge (a card
shows its owner with the initial); no small spaced capitals above titles;
one accent per screen; commas and line breaks separate details, never
rows of dots or dashes, and no em dash in the interface copy; controls
carry real labels; every state says what happened and what to do.

**S8. Navigation.** One web app at every width:

- **Under 640 px:** the header shows the wordmark (a link to Home) and,
  where a page needs it, one action; a bottom tab bar holds **Split**,
  **Household** and **Settings** (the app already switches to a bottom
  bar under 640 px; this keeps the breakpoint).
- **From 640 px:** the header holds the wordmark and the same three
  destinations as a menu, the active one underlined in rust.
- **Routes:** `/` Home, `/split` the split, `/household` a placeholder
  ("Coming in M4: households, members and the expense ledger", with a
  link to the split), `/settings`. The placeholder is navigation
  scaffolding and that one line of copy only: no household state, model
  or storage is added before M4 (O-EXT-3). `/finances` redirects to `/household`:
  the Finances page promised the old finance-file milestone, which the
  2026-09-28 restructure dropped.
- **Home** becomes a short start page: what Settle does in two lines, a
  primary "Split a bill" (or "Continue your bill" when the saved draft
  has content, by S9's `billHasContent` rule), and the privacy line.
- The theme control moves into Settings (system, light, dark), with a
  compact toggle kept in the header from 640 px.

**S9. The split as three steps.** At every width the page has three
steps, **Receipt**, **Who had what** and **The split**, named in a steps
indicator (not "Step 1 of 3"). The step lives in the URL
(`/split?step=receipt|items|split`), so the browser's Back and Forward
move between steps and a reload keeps the step. With no step in the URL,
a bill without content opens on Receipt and a bill with content on Who
had what (L4-I1). A fresh bill is never item-less: `createBill`
(`billReducer.ts`:88) gives it two unnamed people and one empty item
assigned to both, so "content" is not "has items". It is the existing
`billHasContent` (`importUi.ts`:17: more than one item, an item with a
name or price, or a tax, tip or discount that isn't none), the rule the
import already uses to decide whether it replaces anything. The step
default, Home's button (S8) and the tests all use that one function.

- **Receipt:** scan (Take photo, Choose file, drop a file) or Type it in;
  everything M2 and M2.5 do there stays (the support check, the photo
  advice, progress, the errors). Scanning over a bill that already has
  items replaces it as today (`onImported`'s `replaceBill`), with no
  confirmation added or removed (L2-O2).
- **Who had what:** the receipt summary and check, the people (add,
  name, remove), who paid, the items, and the adjustments (tax, tip,
  discount).
- **The split:** the total, each person's share as a bar in their colour,
  who pays whom, and the existing Copy summary.
- **From 1024 px**, the Who had what step shows The split beside the
  items and updates it live; "See the split" is then not shown, and the
  third step is still reachable from the steps indicator. The width
  check is a small `useMediaQuery` hook that answers "narrow" when
  `window.matchMedia` is missing (jsdom), so the tests need no polyfill
  and see the narrow layout.
- **New bill (M-I-1).** Today's New bill button and its hint ("This
  bill is saved on this device until you start a new one.",
  `SplitPage.tsx`:208-232) stay together, below the step's content, on
  **every** step, disabled while a scan runs, as today. Choosing it keeps
  today's confirmation ("Start a new bill? This clears the current one.",
  now from the catalogue); cancelling changes nothing, the step and URL
  included. Confirming does everything it does today (the `newBill`
  action with fresh ids, the receipt summary cleared, the receipt image,
  photo advice, photo checks and read lines cleared, the highlighted row
  cleared, the bill inputs remounted) and then makes **Receipt** the
  active step: the URL becomes `/split?step=receipt` by **replacing** the
  current history entry (`navigate(…, { replace: true })`), not pushing
  one, and focus moves to the Receipt heading. So Back does not return to
  the cleared step with an empty bill. Older entries before it stay, as
  the browser can't remove them; each step works with a fresh bill
  (Type it in already leads to Who had what with one, its one empty
  item ready to fill), so such an entry shows the fresh bill at that
  step, never a stale one.
- **The CI smoke test's contract (L1-I1).** The required `app` check
  runs `app/scripts/check-requests.mjs scan` three times
  (`.github/workflows/app-ci.yml`): it opens `/split` on an empty
  profile, sets the file on the first `input[type=file]`, waits for
  `[data-receipt-check]`, then reads `settle.bill` and `settle.receipt`.
  So (a) the Receipt step, which an empty first load of `/split` shows,
  keeps a file input in the DOM, and (b) a finished import moves to Who
  had what with the receipt check panel rendered (its
  `data-receipt-check` attribute kept) and its heading focused, as
  today. Neither the script nor `app-ci.yml` needs to change. In case one
  does, `.github/workflows/app-ci.yml` is moved into this item's
  implementation-stage protected paths, as M2.5 did.

**S10. Person-first assignment.** On Who had what, a person picker (one
person at a time, a radio group of person chips) sits above the items.
Tapping or pressing Enter or Space on an item row toggles the chosen
person on that item (`aria-pressed` on the row's button; the change is
announced). An "Everyone" action assigns all people to an item. Each row
keeps an **Edit** button that opens today's item editor in place (name,
quantity, unit price, the per-person toggles, custom shares, remove), so
nothing the editor does today is lost and the existing assignment tests
keep a path. Flagged rows ("Check the price") keep their flag, now an
icon plus words.

**S11. English and European Portuguese** (the user's choice on
2026-10-07: full Portuguese in this milestone).

- **No translation library.** `app/src/i18n/en.ts` is the source
  catalogue (nested keys, `as const`); `pt.ts` is typed against it, so a
  missing or extra key fails `tsc`. `t(key, params)` interpolates
  `{name}`-style parameters; plurals use `Intl.PluralRules` with `one`
  and `other` forms. A `LanguageProvider` and `useT()` hook serve the
  current catalogue.
- **The Language setting:** System (default), English, Português. System
  picks the first of `navigator.languages` that starts with `pt` or `en`,
  else English. The choice is stored under `settle.language`; storage
  that can't be read means System, as the theme does. `<html lang>`
  follows (`en` or `pt-PT`), changing without a reload.
- **Language and Region stay separate.** The Region setting (number
  format and currency, M1) is unchanged; a Portuguese interface with
  English number formatting is allowed. Region's defaults stay
  `pt-PT` / `EUR`.
- **Scope of the catalogue:** every text a person sees: labels, buttons,
  headings, hints, the receipt read errors (`messages.ts`), the photo
  advice, the receipt check and Review lines, the result and its copied
  summary, the placeholder pages, `aria-label`s and the document title.
  The rule is every user-visible string in `app/src/` outside
  `src/i18n/`, wherever it lives: in components and also in the plain
  modules that build interface text, today
  `features/split/components/fields.ts` (every validation message, the
  default "Item N" and "Person N" names), `features/receipt/review.ts`
  (the receipt notices), `features/receipt/lineReview.ts` (the line
  roles) and `features/receipt/messages.ts` (the read errors). Those
  modules return a catalogue key with its parameters, or take the `t`
  function, instead of an English sentence (L1-I2).
  The rule covers text kept in constants and maps and shown through an
  expression, not only text written in the markup (M-I-2). Today that
  is `LOCALE_LABELS` and `CURRENCY_LABELS` (`SettingsPage.tsx`:13-24),
  the navigation labels (`NAV_ITEMS`, `Layout.tsx`:5), the theme labels
  (`LABELS`, `ThemeToggle.tsx`:4), the adjustment hints (`HINTS`,
  `AdjustmentsSection.tsx`:23), `ADJUSTMENT_LABELS` (`fields.ts`:132),
  `ROLE_LABELS` (`lineReview.ts`:9), the maps in `messages.ts`, and the
  two `window.confirm` prompts (New bill, `SplitPage.tsx`:215, and
  `REPLACE_PROMPT` in `ScanReceipt.tsx`). Each becomes catalogue keys: a
  map from a code to a key, never to a sentence. Region option labels
  (the locale and currency names) are catalogue text like any other.
  Receipt *parsing* keywords are not interface text and are untouched.
  Item names read from receipts are data and are never translated. The
  web manifest is a static file read before the app runs, so its
  `lang`, name and description stay English.
- **Display text and saved text (L1-I3).** Text is translated when it is
  shown, never stored, with one exception. The default "Item N" and
  "Person N" names, the validation messages, the notices and the copied
  summary are all built at render or copy time in the current language;
  none is saved. The exception is the stand-in item "Not read from the
  receipt" (`NOT_READ_ITEM_NAME`), which an import (`toBill.ts`) or "Add
  the difference" (`SplitPage.tsx`) saves into the bill as an item name.
  It is saved in the language of the moment, like any name a person
  types. It stays recognised by its name, as today, but the name may be
  **either catalogue's** value: one function, `isNotReadItemName(name)`
  in `src/i18n/`, compares with the English and the Portuguese text, and
  `review.ts` (`readShare`, `isUnreadImport`) and `accuracy.ts`
  (`readRows`) call it instead of comparing with `NOT_READ_ITEM_NAME`.
  This keeps today's behaviour exactly, in both languages and across a
  language switch mid-bill: a renamed stand-in counts as a read row, one
  whose price alone changed does not, and a read item that happens to
  carry that name is excluded, as `SplitPage.review.test.tsx`:286 checks
  today. No field is added to the receipt summary, so `receiptStore.ts`
  and the saved shapes are unchanged (L2-I1).
- **Translation:** European Portuguese (pt-PT spelling and vocabulary,
  for example "ecrã", "registo", "partilhar"), the impersonal or implicit
  "you" rather than "tu"/"você" where Portuguese allows it, and the voice
  of `PRODUCT.md` (calm, precise, friendly). Claude translates; the user,
  a native speaker, reviews the Portuguese at the functional review.

## Acceptance targets

- Every screen in the inventory (S13) follows S1 to S7 in both themes at
  360, 390, 1024 and 1440 px, with no horizontal scroll at 360 px.
- `tokens.test.ts` gains a contrast test: every text and fill pair the
  tokens define (both themes, people colours with their initial) is at
  least 4.5:1 (3:1 for the 28 px and larger title sizes and for
  non-text marks such as share bars against the card).
- The app makes no request outside its own origin (the existing CSP and
  request checks), fonts included.
- `pt.ts` covers every key of `en.ts` with the same parameters (compile
  time and a parameter-parity test), and no interface text lives outside
  the catalogue. Three layers check it (M-I-2):
  1. **The literal-text guard**, a test using the TypeScript compiler
     API (already installed). It walks every `.ts` and `.tsx` file under
     `src/` outside `src/i18n/`, minus tests, `*.node.ts` files and an
     exclusion list kept in the test with a reason per entry (the parser
     and its keywords, the reader engines: data and code, not interface
     text). Inclusion is the default, so a new module is checked without
     anyone adding it. It fails on (a) any JSX text with a letter, (b) a
     string-literal `aria-label`, `title`, `placeholder` or `alt`, and
     (c) **any** string literal or template-literal text, in any position
     (a map value, a constant, an array, a function argument such as
     `window.confirm`), that reads as language: a letter followed by a
     space and another letter, or a capital followed by a lower-case
     letter (so "Portuguese (Portugal)", "Home" and "Light" all fail,
     while codes, keys, class names, routes and storage keys such as
     `pt-PT`, `EUR`, `/split` or `settle.bill` pass). Module specifiers,
     literal types, and developer-only text (the arguments of any
