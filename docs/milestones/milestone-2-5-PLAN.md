## Goal

M2 reads receipts with Tesseract.js. On the user's real receipts it
reached only 11–22 % of the items on three Lidl app screenshots and 56 %
on a Continente photo; the user accepted those floors to close M2, on
the condition that a better reader follows. **If people still have to
type in or check the items on a large share of their receipts, receipt
reading doesn't work as a product.**

After this milestone:

- the built-in reader is **PaddleOCR**, still free and entirely in the
  browser: nothing leaves the device;
- on a local test set of **at least 30 real receipts**, at least **94 %**
  of the images are read with **no edit needed** (aiming for 100 %), and
  **no** receipt with a wrong or missing price ever shows "Matches";
- that number comes from one written-down measure (P2), run in a real
  browser, so it can be repeated and compared.

## Evidence (before planning)

**The spike.** A local, unpushed spike (branch `spike/paddleocr`, worktree
`../project-W-paddle-spike`) replaced the reader with PaddleOCR
(`ppu-paddle-ocr` 6.6.0 on `onnxruntime-web` 1.30.0; the PP-OCRv5 mobile
detection model and the PP-OCRv5 Latin recognition model) behind M2's
`ReceiptReader`, with M2's parser unchanged. With R19's local coverage:

| receipt | Tesseract (M2, accepted floor) | PaddleOCR spike | M2R1 plan target |
|---|---|---|---|
| lidl1 (app screenshot, 223 px wide) | 11 % | 44 % | 60 % |
| lidl2 (app screenshot, 223 px wide) | 21 % | 73 % | 60 % |
| lidl3 (app screenshot, 261 px wide) | 22 % | all 5 items | 60 % |
| Continente (photo on a table) | 56 % | 94 % | 75 % |
| Tiffosi (photo on a table) | 100 % | 100 % | "Matches" |

Item names were much cleaner, and a read took 0.5–2 s on the development
machine. The user confirmed the improvement on their phone (not timed).

**What the spike did that this plan must not:**

- It let Vite bundle ONNX Runtime's WebGPU (`jsep`) build: a 28 MB wasm
  (6.7 MB gzipped). The plain SIMD build is 14.2 MB (3.7 MB gzipped).
- Its vendoring script copied every ONNX Runtime variant (83 MB) into
  `public/vendor/ort/`.
- It downloaded the models from Hugging Face at build time with no
  pinned revision or checksum.

Measured sizes of what a first scan needs with the plain build: ONNX
Runtime wasm 14.2 MB (3.7 MB gzipped), detection model 4.7 MB (4.5 MB),
recognition model 8.1 MB (7.3 MB): about **27 MB raw, 15.5 MB gzipped**,
against about 9 MB for Tesseract's.

**The local test set today** (git-ignored `app/src/features/receipt/fixtures/local/`):
nine images of eight distinct receipts. The five from M2 (three Lidl
screenshots, Continente, Tiffosi) have price-only `.expected.json` files
and PNG copies made for Node's PNG-only decoder. The four the user added
on 2026-09-30 are JPEGs with no expected file yet. **All nine are
compressed copies** (the four new ones 900 × 1600, 1.4 MP; the Continente
and Tiffosi originals 1536 × 2048, 3.1 MP; the screenshots 223–261 px
wide): none is a camera original as the app's "Take photo" receives it
(typically about 12 MP, sometimes HEIC). The new four:

- a watch shop: a code line with quantity, VAT and price,
  then the description and two attribute lines (`Marca :`,
  `Classificação AT :`), a fiscal QR code;
- a clothes shop: a name line with quantity, price and total, then
  continuation lines (a variant, two codes), a card-payment slip after the
  total that repeats a `Total` line with the amount, and **no** fiscal QR
  code;
- a restaurant, **photographed twice** (the same receipt, different
  light and angle): the VAT rate printed against the
  quantity (`13%3`, `23%1`), a fiscal QR code.

## Decisions taken in this plan

| # | Decision | Chosen |
|---|----------|--------|
| P1 | The local test set (format v2) | Each case is one `<case>.expected.json` next to its image in `fixtures/local/`. Fields: `image` (the file name, as the phone produced it: JPEG or PNG), `total` (as printed, decimal string), `totalSource` (`qr` or `printed`: what the receipt offers), `items` (each `{ name, price }`: one per row a correct bill holds, the name as printed on the item's first line, and the price the row should carry: its printed line total after its own savings or discount lines, as M2R1's R8 applies them), optional `discount` (a bill-level discount only), optional `tip` and `tax` (a service charge or an added tax, as the bill should hold them, each as an amount, so a charge read as an item row can be told from one read as the tip; a tax already included in the item prices, as Portuguese VAT is, is not a `tax` here, since the bill holds none; no split mode, since the import never sets one: D12 keeps the current bill's tax and tip modes), optional `sameReceiptAs` (another case's name: a second photo of one receipt, like the restaurant's second photo), `part` (P14), and `capture`: `camera` (a full-resolution original, as the phone's camera saved it, JPEG or HEIC), `shared` (a compressed copy, e.g. sent through a messaging app), `screenshot` or `scan`. The five M2 cases move to v2: `image` points at the original JPEG (the PNG copies stay on disk but aren't read), and their item names are added. The folder stays git-ignored and guarded exactly as R17 made it (tracked, staged and history checks). **Drafting the expected files** (the user's decision, 2026-09-30): Claude drafts each one from the image in the working session, which sends the image to the assistant's model service, as the first nine already were; the user checks every draft before it counts. The images and expected files still never enter the repository or a bundle. |
| P2 | One accuracy measure (pure, `accuracy.ts`) | What matters is the split, so a receipt passes on **rows, money and adjustments**, with a loose check that each row is the right product (the user's decisions, 2026-09-30). Per image, after the full import (reader, QR, bill conversion), **no edit needed** means all of: **(a) exactly the rows bought**: the read rows and the expected rows pair one to one, none missing and none extra, where a pair needs the **same line total** and a **recognisable product** (below); a row is one bought item line, so `2 X 4,04 … 8,08` is one row priced 8,08; **(b) the adjustments**: the imported bill's discount, tip and tax each equal the expected file's (P1; an absent field means none), as amounts; an adjustment read as another (a service charge as the tax, a tip as a discount), an adjustment read as an item row, or an item row absorbed into an adjustment, fails (a) or (b) even when the total still closes. Split modes aren't compared: the conversion never sets them (D12 keeps the current bill's `taxMode` and `tipMode`, `toBill.ts`), so comparing them would measure the starting bill, not the reading; **(c) the total**: the trusted total the import settled on (QR or printed) equals the expected total, and the check is "match". **Recognisable product** tolerates ordinary OCR corruption but not a different product at the same price (manual external review round 2, M-I-3). Both names are normalised (lower case, no accents, alphanumerics and single spaces), and OCR's look-alike characters are folded (`0`→`o`, `1`→`l`, `5`→`s`, `8`→`b`). A read name is recognisable when **both** hold: **(i) the whole name is close**: its edit distance to the expected name is at most **1** when the expected name has at most 5 characters, and at most **⌊n/3⌋** for a longer one of n characters; **(ii) every distinctive word is there**: each word of the expected name of at least 4 letters (letters only, and not on a short stop list of common words and units such as `com`, `sem`, `para`, `emb`, `uni`, `und`) occurs somewhere in the read name, spaces ignored, within **max(1, ⌊length/4⌋)** edits (an approximate substring match, so a word OCR split or merged with its neighbour still counts). A shared word is never enough on its own. So `COLA ZER0`, `C0LA ZER` and `COLAZERO` pass for `Cola Zero`, `PA0` for `Pão`, `BIGA` for `Bica`; `SOPA` doesn't pass for `Cola`, `AZEITE` for `Leite`, `BATIDA` for `Batata`, `IOGURTE MANGA` for `Iogurte Morango`, `Tosta com queijo` for `Pão com chouriço`, nor `\|\|\|1 ,:` for anything. These thresholds were checked on those examples; CP1 checks them again on the set's own names (below) and may only tighten them, with a recorded reason, before Tesseract's baseline is taken; after that the measure is fixed, and any change re-scores every recorded number and goes to the user. Pairing is a maximum one-to-one matching over those pairs, with receipt order breaking ties, so rows at the same price can't stand in for each other. Reported per image and in total: **receipt accuracy** (share of images with no edit needed: the target), **distinct-receipt accuracy** (a receipt counts only if every one of its images passes: reported beside it, so several photos of one easy receipt can't inflate the result), **row accuracy** (share of expected rows paired, and the count of extra rows), **name accuracy** (the stricter informational measure, kept separate from recognisability: share of paired rows whose name, normalised but without look-alike folding, equals the printed name exactly), and **false matches**: images whose check says "match" while the money is wrong, i.e. (a) fails with rows paired by line total alone, or (b) or (c) fails. The check compares money only (`reconcile.ts`), so an image whose prices, adjustments and total are right but one name isn't recognisable fails receipt accuracy (it isn't "no edit needed") without being a false match. Quantities aren't compared: the split uses the line total. |
| P3 | Where the numbers come from | **The browser run is authoritative**: `scripts/measure-local.mjs` builds on `check-requests.mjs`'s headless-Brave driver, scans every case with the production build, reads the imported bill and summary from the page's `localStorage` (`settle.bill`, `settle.receipt`) over DevTools, the same data the Node test scores, and scores them with `accuracy.ts`. It prints numbers and case names only (no receipt text) and writes its full report under the git-ignored `.ai-review/`. The Node test (`receipts.local.ocr.test.ts`, now with PaddleOCR) is for fast iteration; CP2 measures how far Node and the browser differ (P6). |
| P4 | The engine | `ppu-paddle-ocr` with `onnxruntime-web`'s **plain SIMD wasm build only** (no WebGPU, no JSPI or asyncify builds), the PP-OCRv5 mobile detection model and the PP-OCRv5 Latin recognition model. Exact versions pinned in `package.json` (no range). ONNX Runtime runs **single-threaded**, set explicitly (`env.wasm.numThreads = 1`, so it never tries to start thread workers): multi-threading needs cross-origin isolation (COOP/COEP headers), which static hosts (M6) usually can't send. Vite must not emit any ONNX Runtime wasm into `dist/assets/`: the runtime is loaded from `vendor/ort/` through `env.wasm.wasmPaths`, and a build test fails if `dist/` holds a second copy. |
| P5 | The models: where they come from | Downloaded at build time by `scripts/vendor-paddle.mjs` from the `ppu-paddle-ocr` model mirror on Hugging Face, **pinned to a commit revision in the URL and to a SHA-256 per file**; a mismatch fails the build. Cached in the git-ignored `app/.paddle-models/`, and in CI with `actions/cache` keyed on the pinned hashes. Never committed to Git. The mirror's licence and the models' origin (PaddleOCR, Apache-2.0) are checked in CP2 before anything depends on them. These are PaddleOCR's own two small network files (text detection and text recognition), run in the browser; they're part of the local route, not an AI service, and nothing is sent anywhere. |
| P6 | The reader in the app | `paddleReader.ts` implements `ReceiptReader` (id `paddle`) and runs the whole OCR in a **dedicated module worker** (`paddle.worker.ts`), as Tesseract did, so the page stays responsive. Pages go to the worker as transferred pixel buffers. The model loads once, on the first scan, with D17's phases (`loadingReader`, then `reading` with progress per page). Cancelling terminates the worker (the next scan starts a new one and reloads the models from the HTTP cache). Every D16 error code keeps its meaning: a failed runtime or model fetch is `assetsUnavailable`, an inference failure `ocrFailed`. PDF text layers are still read with no OCR (D6). The OCR itself lives in a worker-independent module, `paddleEngine.ts`, which the worker wraps; in Node, `importDeps.node.ts` calls the same `paddleEngine.ts` directly, with `onnxruntime-web`'s wasm build under Node and a canvas shim for image resizing; CP2 records, per case, whether Node and the browser read the same lines, and the browser stays the reference (P3). |
| P7 | Lines from PaddleOCR | PaddleOCR returns boxes with text. They're assembled into `TextLine`s by vertical overlap, left to right, with the confidence of the weakest box (0–100, as D3's `TextLine` expects) and the spike's number repair (`1 ,39` → `1,39`). A box far to the right of the previous one is joined with a wide gap marker the parser can use to tell a price column from a name (CP3 decides from the set whether it helps). |
| P8 | Image clean-up | M2R1's clean-up (R1 text-size scaling, R2 flattening, R20 strips) was tuned for Tesseract. CP2 starts PaddleOCR on the decoded page with only EXIF-aware orientation (as today) and a resize to the detector's input rules; CP3 then re-introduces, **only where the set shows a gain**, text-size upscaling for small screenshots and tiling for long receipts whose text the detector's downscale would shrink below about 10 px. What isn't used any more is deleted, not left dormant. |
| P9 | Parser rules | M2's parser and M2R1's rules stay, re-checked on PaddleOCR's lines. New rules come only from layouts in the set, each with an **invented** synthetic unit test (never real receipt text in a committed file). Known from the four new receipts: a VAT rate printed against the quantity (`13%3`); a code line carrying quantity and price with the description on the next line and attribute lines after it (`Marca :`, `Classificação AT :`) that are never items; an item line followed by continuation lines (a variant, codes) that are never items; a card-payment slip after the total whose `Total` line is never a second total. |
| P10 | Tesseract | **Removed** once PaddleOCR is at least as good as Tesseract on every **tuning** case of the set and every corpus sample (CP4; held-out cases stay unscored until CP6, P14): its worker, cores and language models leave `public/vendor/`, and `tesseract.js` and its data packages leave `package.json`. Until then it stays the default in the committed app, and PaddleOCR is selected with a build flag, so every intermediate commit is shippable. (Default kept by the user, 2026-09-30; a fallback would only help if PaddleOCR failed to load, which a same-origin Tesseract load would likely share.) |
| P11 | Help with images that are too small | When the text in an image is too small to read reliably even after upscaling (the estimated character height, from M2R1's `estimateTextHeight`, under a threshold CP4 sets from the set), the import says so in the scan status, with advice: take a full-resolution screenshot, or zoom in before taking it. It's **not stored**: it's shown for the import that produced it, so the saved summary's format doesn't change (see "Migration"). |
| P12 | Download size | The first scan downloads the runtime and both models once; the browser's HTTP cache keeps them (offline caching stays M6's). **Budget: 30 MB raw** for everything a scan fetches, runtime and models together (about 27 MB measured); over it is a stop condition. |
| P13 | Speed | Measured on **named images**, as the time the user waits for the **whole import** (decoding, the reader, the QR scan and the bill conversion: from choosing the file to the check panel), with the reader already loaded; the first load (runtime and models) is measured and reported separately. The images: **(a)** the largest camera original in the set (P1's `camera`, at least 12 MP), **(b)** the Tiffosi photo as received (1536 × 2048, 3.1 MP), **(c)** lidl1 (223 × 1600). **Engineering gate**, the development machine's browser: (a) in at most **5 s**. **Phone** (the user's, the preview build over the local network): (a) and (b) within **20 s**, (c) within **10 s**. M2R1's R21 named "the 12.6-MP photo", which was the Tiffosi photo *after* Tesseract's ×2 enlargement (M2R1 plan, R1 and CP1), not an input; these replace it. The thresholds tighten R21's 60 s and 20 s (waived for Tesseract), set from the spike's 0.5–2 s on the desktop and a phone typically being 3–8× slower (default kept by the user, 2026-09-30). |
| P14 | Tuning without fooling ourselves | Rules tuned on the same receipts they're measured on can look better than they are. So the set has two parts: **tuning cases** (every case present when CP3 starts, and any added during CP3) and **held-out cases**: at least **10 distinct receipts** the user adds after CP3's last change, scored in CP6 without any change made for them. The target applies to the whole set; the held-out part's receipt accuracy is reported beside it, and falling more than 10 points below the tuning part's is a stop condition (the rules don't generalise). The expected file records its part (`"part": "tuning"` or `"heldOut"`). **Protecting them:** held-out cases are **not scored before CP6's measurement**: both measurements skip them unless run with an explicit `--held-out` flag, used only in CP6. CP4 and CP5 may not change reading, and their "numbers unchanged" checks run on the tuning cases only. If any reading change is made after a held-out case has been scored, every scored held-out case becomes a tuning case, and the 10-held-out requirement has to be met again with new receipts. |
| P15 | The row-by-row review | Next to the check panel, "Review lines" shows what the reader found: the receipt image with a box over each line it read, coloured by the role the parser gave it (**item**, **item detail** such as a code or variant line, **discount or savings**, **total or subtotal**, **tax table**, **payment**, **ignored**), and the same lines as a list, in receipt order, each with its role, its text and its amount. Selecting a line in the list highlights its box and the matching bill row. An **ignored** or **item detail** line with an amount has **"Add as item"**, which adds it as a named, priced, flagged item shared by everyone, through M2R1's existing `addItems` action (all-or-nothing under the 100-item limit). **"Add a missed line"** adds an item the reader didn't see at all (a name and a price) the same way. Nothing here is stored: like "Show receipt image", the review exists only for the import that produced it, and a reload drops it (see "Migration"). A PDF read from its text layer has no image boxes, so it shows the list only. The list is the accessible form; the boxes are a visual aid. |

## Acceptance targets

- **The local test set** has at least **30 distinct receipts** when CP6
  measures it (more photos of the same receipt are extra cases, not extra
  receipts), at least 10 of them held out (P14), **at least 10 cases
  that are camera originals** (P1's `camera`, full resolution as the
  phone saved them, including at least one HEIC if the user's phone saves
  HEIC), with a variety of kinds: supermarkets, restaurants and
  cafés, shops, app screenshots and phone photos (crumpled, long or at an
  angle).
- **Receipt accuracy ≥ 94 %** of the images, aiming for 100 %, measured
  by P2 in the browser (P3), on the whole set; the held-out part within
  10 points of the tuning part (P14). In counts: with 30–33 images at
  most one may fail (29/30 = 96.7 %, 28/30 = 93.3 %), with 34–49 at most
  two, with 50–66 at most three. Distinct-receipt accuracy (P2) is
  reported beside it, with no separate target.
- **Zero false matches.**
- **Row accuracy and name accuracy** are reported per case and in total;
  they carry no separate target: receipt accuracy already requires every
  row right, and names don't decide a pass (P2).
- **The committed synthetic corpus** (samples 01–13 and CP4's new ones)
  passes in CI with PaddleOCR. Samples keep their current result unless a
  sample's expected file was itself wrong, which is recorded.
- **P12's download budget, P13's engineering gate and phone times.**
- **Privacy:** `check-requests.mjs` passes on the new reader (only
  same-origin `GET`s for build files, no receipt value in any request, no
  CSP violation).
- **When the targets are checked:** CP1 records Tesseract's baseline with
  P2 on the set as it is then; CP2 records PaddleOCR's first numbers with
  no tuning; CP3 records the numbers after each change it keeps; CP6
  runs the final measurement on the full set. A target that isn't met is
  a **stop condition** (below): the numbers and causes go to the user,
  who decides. The targets are never lowered silently.

### CP1 — The local test set and the accuracy measure

**Requirements:** REQ-1, REQ-2

**Files**
- `app/src/features/receipt/accuracy.ts` (new, pure): P2's measure, and
  `nameMatches` (normalisation and edit distance). Replaces
  `matchedCoverage.ts`, whose R19 coverage is kept as one of its outputs
  (item accuracy by amount) so M2's numbers stay comparable.
- `app/src/features/receipt/localFixtures.node.ts`: loading format v2
  (P1), validating each expected file (a missing image, a bad amount, an
  unknown `sameReceiptAs` fail with the case's name), and counting
  distinct receipts. R17's git guards are unchanged.
- `app/src/features/receipt/encodePage.node.ts` or a new
  `decodeImage.node.ts`: JPEG decoding in Node (`jpeg-js`, MIT, dev
  dependency only), with EXIF orientation applied as the browser does, by
  a small reader of the orientation tag (`jpeg-js` ignores EXIF).
- `app/src/features/receipt/receipts.local.ocr.test.ts`: reads v2 cases
  (JPEG or PNG), scores them with `accuracy.ts`, logs numbers only, and
  checks each case's own optional floor, `minRowAccuracy` (the share of
  expected rows read with the right price). The five M2 cases start from
  their M2 floors, whose `minCoverage` measured matched money over the
  total: CP1 converts each by measuring row accuracy with Tesseract on
  that case and taking the lower of that and the old coverage floor, so
  no case can pass today and fail the guard. Held-out cases are skipped
  unless the `--held-out` flag is given (P14).
- `app/scripts/measure-local.mjs` (new): P3's browser run. Shares the
  headless-Brave and preview-server code with `check-requests.mjs` (moved
  into a small module both import; `check-requests.mjs`'s behaviour and
  audit format don't change).
- The local expected files (git-ignored, never in a bundle): the five
  M2 cases converted, and the four new ones drafted and checked (P1).
- `app/README.md`, "Local real-receipt fixtures": format v2, how to add a
  receipt, how to run both measurements.
- Tests next to each file.

**Tests**
- `accuracy.ts`: an exact read; a missing row; an extra row; a wrong
  price; a quantity row counted as one row; **same-price substitution**
  (Coffee 2,50 and Croissant 2,50 expected, Coffee and an unrelated 2,50
  row read: fails); **duplicate same-price rows** all read correctly
  (passes, paired in order); a badly OCR'd but recognisable name
  (`C0LA ZER0`, `C0LA ZER`, `COLAZERO`: pass, and count against strict
  name accuracy only); a short name with one OCR error (`BIGA` for
  `Bica`, `PA0` for `Pão`: pair); **unrelated short names at the same
  price within the old half-length allowance** (`SOPA` for `Cola`,
  `AZEITE` for `Leite`, `BATIDA` for `Batata`: don't pair); two products
  sharing a word that isn't on the stop list but otherwise different
  (`Iogurte Manga` for `Iogurte Morango`, `Iogurte Natural` for
  `Iogurte Morango`: don't pair); two unrelated names sharing only a
  stop-list word (`com`) at the same price (don't pair); an unrelated or
  garbled name at the right price (fails); a long name with a few OCR
  errors and a split or merged word (pairs); the bill-level
  adjustments: right items and total but the wrong
  tip amount, the tip read as a discount, a service charge read as the
  tax, the wrong discount (each fails); the bill's split modes differing
  from the starting bill's defaults changes nothing (modes aren't
  compared); an adjustment read as an item row, and an item row absorbed
  into an adjustment, both with the total still closing (each fails);
  accents and spacing; distinct-receipt accuracy over two images of one
  receipt (one fails: the receipt fails); a false match (a missing and an
  extra item that cancel out, check "match": counted); a name-only
  failure (every price, adjustment and the total right, one unrecognisable
  name, check "match": fails receipt accuracy, **not** counted as a false
  match); the "Not read from the receipt" stand-in item never counts as a
  read item.
- **The name rule on the set's own names** (local, never committed; numbers
  only): every pair of *different* expected names in the set is compared,
  and none may be recognisable as the other (two genuinely identical
  products on different receipts are listed, not counted); and the rows
  Tesseract reads at the right price are listed with whether they pair,
  so a rule that rejects ordinary OCR noise shows up before the baseline
  is recorded. The thresholds are only tightened from this, never loosened

## Out of scope

  roadmap keeps BYOK in the backlog as the user's decision if the local

  "Useful"): not in this plan, since PaddleOCR's measured numbers leave
  it no clear case yet; it can be raised if CP3 plateaus.
- Cropping and perspective correction, unless CP3's numbers show angled
  photos failing (then it's proposed to the user as a plan amendment).
- WebGPU and multi-threaded inference (P4).
- Offline caching of the reader (M6).
